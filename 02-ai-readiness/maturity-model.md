# The AI readiness model

Six dimensions, five questions each, scored 1-5 on evidence. The model is deliberately small: a leadership team should be able to argue with every line in one meeting. The blank questionnaire is in the [playbook](../09-playbook/templates/readiness-questionnaire.md).

## Levels

| Level | Name | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exploring | No structure; individuals try tools on their own |
| 2 | Experimenting | Some pilots and awareness; no common approach, no measurement |
| 3 | Formalising | Policy, ownership and a repeatable way to deliver and measure |
| 4 | Scaling | Several AI solutions in production, monitored, governed, with value tracked |
| 5 | Transforming | AI is part of how the company plans, works and competes |

## Dimensions

| Dimension | The question behind it |
|---|---|
| **Strategy & leadership** | Is AI tied to business goals, owned and funded? |
| **Data foundations** | Is the data owned, usable, reachable and good enough to evaluate AI on? |
| **Technology & tooling** | Is there a platform to build, run and watch AI solutions, and sanctioned tools for staff? |
| **Governance, risk & compliance** | Are there rules, an inventory, risk classification and human oversight? |
| **People & skills** | Do people understand AI, are there champions and owners, is the works council involved? |
| **Process & operations** | Are processes documented and measured, so AI has something to improve and a baseline to beat? |

## How we score

1. **Evidence, not opinion.** Every score cites something we saw, counted or were shown. "We are quite data-driven" is not evidence; "14% duplicate customer records in a sample of 200" is.
2. **Two scorers.** The consultant and a client counterpart score separately, then reconcile. Differences of two points or more are discussed in the readout.
3. **Targets are a decision, not a wish.** The 12-month target per item was set in workshop 4 against the roadmap: a target only rises if a roadmap item pays for it.
4. **Gaps are ranked by what they block.** A gap matters first when it is large *and* the top use cases depend on it ([gap analysis](gap-analysis.md)).

## Why not a bigger framework?

Large maturity frameworks (dozens of capabilities) are good for benchmarking across companies and bad at producing decisions in a 250-person firm. This model maps onto them: strategy and governance onto ISO/IEC 42001 clauses 5-6, data and technology onto the usual data-management capability areas, people onto the AI-literacy duty in Article 4 of the EU AI Act.
